## 1. Executive Summary & Problem Formulation

Modern enterprise networks and cloud environments face sophisticated adversaries—ranging from Human-Operated Ransomware (HoR) campaigns to Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs). Unlike automated script-based intrusions, sophisticated multi-stage attacks unfold over days, weeks, or months, employing evasive Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures (TTPs) aligned with frameworks like the MITRE ATT&CK Matrix and the Lockheed Martin Cyber Kill Chain. 

### The Problem Statement
> **"Mining Attack Sequences and Missing-Event Rules to Predict the Next Attacker Step"**

Existing detection and prediction systems exhibit three fundamental vulnerabilities:
1. **The Semantic & Heterogeneity Gap**: Real enterprise security alerts stem from disparate, independent sensors (Endpoint Detection and Response [EDR], Network Intrusion Detection Systems [NIDS], Web Application Firewalls [WAFs], and Host Anomaly Miners). As established by Roelofs et al. (ACM SAC 2024) and Landauer et al. (CSET 2024), over 99% of raw alerts are noise or false positives, obscuring the underlying attack progression.
2. **The "Missing-Event" Blind Spot**: Most sequence learning and alert correlation models (e.g., traditional Markov models, standard LSTMs, attack graphs) strictly assume *positive event presence*—i.e., every transition requires an observed alert. However, modern adversaries deliberately bypass hooks using direct system calls (e.g., Sysmon/EDR evasion in MalAF, Liu et al., IEEE TDSC 2024), execute fileless/living-off-the-land binaries (LotL), or operate in sensor blind spots. When expected intermediate events do not trigger, standard sequence models fail to correlate subsequent actions or misclassify the attack path.
3. **Reactive vs. Proactive Predictive Foretelling**: Conventional intrusion detection systems produce retrospective alarms once a critical host has already been compromised. Proactive defense requires *foretelling the next attacker step* ($T_{k+1}$) with calibrated confidence scores before high-impact objectives (credential dumping, lateral movement, data exfiltration, or encryption) occur.

This report synthesizes the foundational research corpus collected in this workspace—drawing directly on **Roelofs et al. (2024)**, **Meng et al. ($M^2ASK$, 2024)**, **Landauer et al. (AIT-ADS, 2024)**, **Buchta et al. (2024)**, **Li et al. (DeepAG, 2023)**, **Liu et al. (MalAF, 2024)**, **Ouyang et al. (2026)**, and **Khan et al. (2026)**—and presents an end-to-end, mathematically rigorous architecture for mining attack sequences, extracting missing-event rules, and predicting upcoming attacker steps.

### 2.1 Multi-Sensor Alert Fusion & Noise Robustness
* **Roelofs et al. (SAC 2024, ING Bank & TU Delft)**: Demonstrates that isolated detection engines produce staggering false-positive rates (up to 176 alerts/host/day; 99% FPs). They deploy an Integration Layer (IL) combining weak signals across EDR, NTA, SIEM, and proxy telemetry into host-day "data bursts". Crucially, they prove that training supervised models (XGBoost) on **Incident Response Team (IRT) final verdicts** rather than noisy first-tier SOC escalations slashes false positives from 31 down to 2, yielding a Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) of 0.998. They utilize SHAP waterfall representations for explainability.
* **Landauer, Skopik, & Wurzenberger (CSET 2024, AIT-ADS)**: Introduced the 2.6-million alert benchmark generated by Wazuh, Suricata, and AMiner across eight multi-step attack scenarios. They formulate mathematical metrics for detector evaluation:
  $$s_{rob}(A, D) = \frac{1}{|S|} \sum_{S} \left( 1 - \min\left(1, \frac{\#(A_{D,S} \text{ in } \Delta_{T,S})}{\#(A_{D,S} \text{ in } \Delta_{A,S})} \cdot \frac{\Delta_{A,S}}{\Delta_{T,S}}\right) \right)$$
  $$s_{det}(D) = \max_{A} \left( s_{rob}(A, D) \cdot \frac{\#(S : A \in S \wedge \#(A_{D,S} \text{ in } \Delta_{A,S}) > 0)}{\#(S : A \in S)} \right)$$
  Their AECID framework achieves a 98.93% alert reduction rate by grouping co-occurring alerts (2-second interval window) into meta-alerts.

### 2.2 Semantic Tagging & Cyber Kill Chain Mapping
* **Meng et al. ($M^2ASK$, ACM CCS 2024)**: Bridges low-level NIDS alerts to MITRE ATT&CK tactics/techniques using NLP embeddings (UMBC cybersecurity word2vec) and DBSCAN clustering, achieving F1-scores of 93.4% (tactics) and 98.4% (techniques). They define formal graph relationship rules:
  1. *Parallel actions*: $a_i.src = a_j.src \wedge a_i.dst = a_j.dst \wedge a_i.stage = a_j.stage$.
  2. *Parent-child actions*: Sequential stage progression ($a_i.stage < a_j.stage$ and $a_i.start < a_j.start$) or lateral transfer ($a_i.dst = a_j.src$).
* **Khan et al. (ACM DTRAP 2026, King Fahd Univ.)**: Formulates sequence learning over ordered 14-dimensional MITRE ATT&CK tactic vectors for 15 Human-Operated Ransomware families. Utilizing SMOTE to counteract extreme class imbalance, they establish that recurrent sequence models (Simple RNN, LSTM, GRU) attain 82% accuracy and 0.9694 AUC. Crucially, they conduct **stage-wise progressive inference**: evaluating model predictions when only stages 1–$k$ are partially observed, demonstrating that detection accuracy reaches 47.85% as early as Privilege Escalation (Stage 6) before the destructive Impact phase.

### 2.3 Deep Predictive Modeling & Concurrency Disambiguation
* **Li et al. (DeepAG, IEEE TDSC 2023)**: Overcomes uni-directional LSTM bias by introducing **Bi-Directional Deep Learning** for attack paths. They utilize a Transformer encoder for parallel semantic vectorization of log sentences, followed by forward and backward LSTMs. They solve the critical concurrency/branching problem: when multiple tasks execute simultaneously, predicted keys branch out. DeepAG validates true concurrency by verifying if branch order permutations maintain monotonically increasing conditional probabilities and converge back to a join node.
* **Liu et al. (MalAF, IEEE TDSC 2024)**: Solves the path-explosion problem in fine-grained API/event logs by introducing:
  1. Parameter-sensitivity event filtering via TF-IDF and GS-DMM Dirichlet multinomial mixture clustering.
  2. Pearson correlation clustering to combine concurrent atomic events into multi-stage attack slots $C_t = (e_1, \dots, e_w)$.
  3. Dynamic Heterogeneous Graph Sequences (MDHGS) with 6 entity types and 6 relation types, aggregated using Dynamic Graph Attention Networks (D-GATs).
  4. Model-free **Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL)** with Greedy Adaptive Dictionary (GAD) learning to extract the adversary's *intrinsic attack preference vector* $h_{pre}$, achieving 96.9%–98.8% path foretelling accuracy.
* **Ouyang et al. (IEEE ISBDAS 2026)**: Presents dynamic Bayesian node compromise probability estimation:
  $$P(v = \text{compromised}) = \min\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^m l_i \cdot r_i}{\sum_{i=1}^m l_i + \epsilon}, 1.0\right)$$
  where $l_i$ is severity and $r_i \in \{0, 0.5, 1\}$ denotes outcome certainty, combined with a composite path confidence score:
  $$Score(P) = \alpha \cdot P_{avg} + \beta \cdot W_{trans} - \gamma \cdot T_{diff}$$

## 3. The Core Innovation: Missing-Event Rule Mining

In multi-stage attack forecasting, existing systems fail when an expected intermediate step is silent. We conceptualize **Missing-Event Rules** as *negative association patterns* and *counterfactual state transitions*.

```
Observed Progression:
[Initial Access (T1566)] =====> [?] (NO EXPLOIT ALERT) =====> [Privilege Escalation (T1068)]
                                       |
                   MISSING-EVENT RULE INFERENCE:
                   Rule: {T1566} ^ ~{T1059 (Execution Alert)} => {LotL Memory Injection / Zero-Day}
                   Expected Next Action: Credential Dumping (T1003) via LSASS with 89% Confidence
```

### 3.1 Formal Definition of Missing-Event Rules
Let $\mathcal{E} = \{e_1, e_2, \dots, e_M\}$ be the universal alphabet of observable security meta-alerts and ATT&CK techniques. An attack sequence observed on asset $h$ up to time $t$ is denoted as:
$$S_t^{(h)} = \langle (a_1, \tau_1), (a_2, \tau_2), \dots, (a_k, \tau_k) \rangle, \quad a_i \in \mathcal{E}, \quad \tau_i \le t$$

Let $\mathcal{M} \subset \mathcal{E}$ denote an expected intermediate attack event (e.g., standard file dropper or command interpreter execution). A **Missing-Event Rule** is formally represented as an implication:
$$R_{missing}: \quad \big( X \wedge \neg \mathcal{M} \big) \xrightarrow{[\text{supp}, \text{conf}, \Delta \tau]} Y$$
where:
* $X \subset \mathcal{E}$ is the set of observed antecedent events (e.g., Suspicious Phishing Attachment, anomalous ingress connection).
* $\neg \mathcal{M}$ represents the **verified non-occurrence** of event $\mathcal{M}$ within temporal constraint $\tau_{X} < t < \tau_{X} + \Delta \tau$.
* $Y \in \mathcal{E}$ is the predicted next attacker action (e.g., Credential Dumping, C&C beaconing).
* $\text{conf} = P(Y \mid X, \neg \mathcal{M})$ is the rule confidence.

### 3.2 Differentiating True Omission from Sensor Packet Loss
A major scientific hurdle in negative event mining is differentiating between:
1. **Adversarial Evasion / LotL**: The attacker deliberately bypassed detection (e.g., memory injection, living-off-the-land utilities).
2. **Telemetry Loss / Sensor Blind Spot**: Network packet drop, EDR sensor buffer overflow, or unconfigured logging policy.

To resolve this, we formulate the **Sensor Visibility Index (SVI)**:
$$\text{SVI}(h, \text{sensor}_j, \Delta \tau) = \frac{\# \text{Benign Heartbeats / Syscalls recorded on } h \text{ by sensor}_j \text{ in } \Delta \tau}{\mathbb{E}[\text{Expected Baseline Rate}]}$$
If $\text{SVI}(h, \text{sensor}_j, \Delta \tau) > \theta_{active}$ (e.g., 0.95), the telemetry stream is active and operational. Therefore, $\neg \mathcal{M}$ is attributed to **stealthy adversarial avoidance**, triggering the negative-sequence rule engine rather than a null-data penalty.

## 4. End-to-End System Architecture

The proposed system consists of five tightly coupled modules operating in a continuous pipeline:

```
[ Multi-Source Ingestion ] (Wazuh HIDS, Suricata NIDS, Proxy Logs, Linux Auditd / Sysmon)
            |
            v
[ Phase 1: Robust Alert Reduction & SVI Filtering ] (s_rob, s_det scoring, DBSCAN, SVI Tracking)
            |
            v
[ Phase 2: CTI & ATT&CK Semantic Sequence Builder ] (UMBC Embeddings + D-GAT Meta-Graph Walks)
            |
            v
[ Phase 3: Missing-Event Rule Mining Engine ] (Negative PrefixSpan + Counterfactual Causal Graphs)
            |
            v
[ Phase 4: Bi-Directional Sequence & Preference Foreteller ] (Bi-LSTM + IRL Q-Learning Optimization)
            |
            v
[ Phase 5: Actionable Incident Prioritization & SHAP Explainability ] (MCC Calibrated, Top-k Next Steps)
```

### Module 1: Ingestion, Robustness Filtering, and Meta-Alert Aggregation
* Ingests heterogeneous data streams (as verified in AIT-ADS and Roelofs et al.): Wazuh host logs, Suricata network flows, AMiner anomaly outputs, and web-proxy traffic.
* Evaluates detector robustness $s_{rob}(A, D)$ and detection power $s_{det}(D)$ across time windows, filtering low-quality alert signatures ($s_{det} < 0.7$).
* Applies a 2-second sliding window pairwise collation combined with DBSCAN on alert textual descriptors to compress duplicate notifications into normalized meta-alerts.

### Module 2: MITRE ATT&CK Mapping & Dynamic Graph State Modeling
* Automatically maps each meta-alert to ATT&CK tactics and techniques using an ensemble text-classification pipeline ($M^2ASK$).
* Constructs the **Malware Dynamic Heterogeneous Graph Sequence (MDHGS)** connecting entities (Host, Process, File, Socket, Registry, User).
* Disambiguates concurrent multi-threaded attacker actions using DeepAG's conditional probability test over permutation sequences, preventing false forks in the execution trajectory.

### Module 3: Missing-Event Rule Mining Engine
* Employs an extended **Negative Sequential Pattern Mining (NSPM)** algorithm (e.g., Neg-PrefixSpan) constrained by time-delta bounds.
* Mines patterns of the form $\langle a_1, \neg a_2, a_3 \rangle$. When an attacker executes $a_1$ and reaches state $a_3$ without generating $a_2$, the system identifies the specific stealth tradecraft (e.g., unhooked direct system calls, PowerShell bypass).
* Computes confidence scores $P(Y \mid X, \neg \mathcal{M})$ calibrated against historical baseline logs.

### Module 4: Bi-Directional Sequence & Intrinsic Preference Foretelling
* Combines **Bi-Directional LSTM (DeepAG)** with **Model-Free Inverse Reinforcement Learning (MalAF)**:
  1. *Forward-Backward Validation*: The forward model predicts candidate next steps $T_{k+1}$ from the observed sequence $S_{1:k}$. The backward model verifies that reversing the generated $h$-step branch reproduces the observed history with high conditional probability.
  2. *Adversary Preference Conditioning*: Incorporates the adversary family's intrinsic preference vector $h_{pre}$ learned via model-free Q-learning:
     $$\hat{C}_{T+1} = \sigma_d\left( W_d \cdot [h_T \parallel h_{pre} \parallel \mathbf{v}_{missing}] + b_d \right)$$
     where $\mathbf{v}_{missing}$ is the latent vector encoding active missing-event rules.

### Module 5: Prioritization, Explainability & Incident Response
* Computes composite path confidence using Ouyang's linear formulation:
  $$\text{Confidence}(P) = \alpha \cdot P_{\text{compromise}} + \beta \cdot W_{\text{transition}} - \gamma \cdot T_{\text{latency}} + \delta \cdot \text{Score}_{\text{missing}}$$
* Translates the predicted step into a concrete security capability (e.g., "Imminent Credential Dumping via Mimikatz on Domain Controller $H_2$").
* Employs SHAP waterfall plots and counterfactual rule explanations so SOC analysts understand why a step was predicted even in the absence of intermediate alerts.

### Detailed Algorithm: Missing-Event Sequence Predictor (MESP)

```python
# Algorithmic Skeleton: Missing-Event Sequence Predictor
def predict_next_attacker_step(observed_sequence, sensor_health_map, rule_base, bi_lstm_model):
    """
    Inputs:
      observed_sequence: List of tuples (event_id, timestamp, asset_id)
      sensor_health_map: Dict mapping (asset_id, sensor_type) -> SVI score
      rule_base: Extracted Missing-Event Rules { (X, ~M): (Y, confidence) }
      bi_lstm_model: Trained Bi-Directional LSTM predictor
    Output:
      predicted_step, confidence_score, explanation
    """
    # 1. Check for expected intermediate events that are missing
    active_missing_features = []
    for antecedent, missing_event, consequent, conf in rule_base:
        if antecedent in observed_sequence and missing_event not in observed_sequence:
            # Verify sensor was healthy (not a dropped packet)
            if sensor_health_map.get_svi(observed_sequence.asset_id) > 0.90:
                active_missing_features.append((missing_event, consequent, conf))
                
    # 2. Forward Bi-LSTM Inference
    seq_vector = vectorize_sequence(observed_sequence)
    forward_candidates = bi_lstm_model.forward_predict(seq_vector)
    
    # 3. Reconcile with Missing-Event Rules
    adjusted_scores = {}
    for candidate, prob in forward_candidates.items():
        boost = sum(c_conf for m, c_target, c_conf in active_missing_features if c_target == candidate)
        adjusted_scores[candidate] = prob * (1.0 + boost)
        
    # 4. Backward LSTM Verification (DeepAG principle)
    top_candidate = max(adjusted_scores, key=adjusted_scores.get)
    backward_verified = bi_lstm_model.backward_verify(observed_sequence, top_candidate)
    
    return top_candidate, adjusted_scores[top_candidate], active_missing_features
```

## 6. Evaluation Framework & Experimental Protocol

Following the rigorous standards of Roelofs et al., Landauer et al., and Khan et al., evaluation must avoid deceptive metrics like standard accuracy on heavily skewed cybersecurity datasets.

### Primary Metrics
1. **Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC)**:
   $$\text{MCC} = \frac{TP \times TN - FP \times FN}{\sqrt{(TP + FP)(TP + FN)(TN + FP)(TN + FN)}}$$
   Accounts for severe class imbalance where attacks represent $< 0.1\%$ of events.
2. **Precision-Recall Area Under Curve (PR-AUC)**: More informative than ROC-AUC when evaluating low-frequency intrusions.
3. **Alert Reduction Rate (ARR)**:
   $$\text{ARR} = \left( 1 - \frac{| \text{Meta-Alerts / Actions Presented to SOC} |}{| \text{Raw Alerts Ingested} |} \right) \times 100\%$$
   Targeting $\ge 98.5\%$ reduction, aligned with AECID and $M^2ASK$.
4. **Stage-Wise Early Detection Latency**: Evaluating model accuracy as a function of attack progression $k \in \{1, \dots, 14\}$ (Khan et al.), ensuring high detection confidence before Stage 7 (Credential Access / Lateral Movement).
